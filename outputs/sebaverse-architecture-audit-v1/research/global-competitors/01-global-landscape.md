## How to interpret the map

Three product families matter: structured adaptive courseware, conversational tutors embedded in learning workflows, and curriculum/exam platforms with support layers. Providers can span families. Compare the learning function and measured behavior, not just whether a product markets itself as AI.

For the founder, the strongest study set is Eedi for diagnostic pedagogy, ALEKS for prerequisite-sensitive assessment, CENTURY/Qubena for guided pathways, Khanmigo for orchestration, Duolingo for instrumented practice/voice, and Squirrel for error-analysis workflows. “Strongest study set” means useful public evidence and distinct mechanisms, not a proven worldwide winner.

Arabic support needs several separate checks: native literacy content, MSA text, Egyptian dialect explanation, child speech recognition, speech synthesis, bilingual mathematical terminology and curriculum alignment. None can be inferred from the others. Regional competitors prevent assuming that Arabic content or parent communication alone forms a defensible advantage.

## Commercial comparison boundary

Numerical offers verified from public pages are recorded in 02; exact local checkout and institutional quotes remain unresolved. Different models—nonprofit access, family subscriptions, per-course licensing, school contracts and supervised centers—carry different content, support and acquisition costs. Sticker price does not reveal marginal AI cost or profitability. Broad international availability does not establish direct consumer access in Egypt.

No patents were treated as evidence of deployed functionality. No paid app or subscription was accessed. Further providers should be added only when a distinct technical disclosure or local learning advantage changes a decision; a longer logo list alone would not improve architecture planning.
