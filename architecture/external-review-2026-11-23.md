Proposed priority means review sequencing, not a founder-approved requirement change. Existing gates still control implementation.

## Sequencing and architectural implications

The review rightly identifies a product risk: no tutoring turn or learning outcome has yet been demonstrated. Root's current tests protect foundational behavior; they do not establish pedagogical value. Avoid spending another unbounded cycle on hypothetical hardening. Finish the current bounded recovery checkpoint, keep F3 qualification blockers explicit, and plan the smallest authorized multi-subject teaching slice under the existing roadmap. No revised release date or margin follows from the report.

The original A0–A7 architecture map and development F0–F8 gates are distinct planning layers. A recommendation to label a voice probe “A1” does not make it eligible under current F3. No paid cap exists by implication: the approved current provider budget is zero. No framework swap, Pipecat dependency, SQLite substitute or prototype migration is justified by supplied evidence.

Open decisions: exact spoken normalization representation and permitted equivalences; subject language defaults; child authentication/recovery route; whether catalog defaults need a new entity; whether to request a separate authorized voice experiment. These remain visible here rather than silently changing settled architecture or the requirement register.
