## Authority and scope

- Canonical withdrawal derives the learner from the consent relationship, locks that learner, reloads and locks the same consent with the same learner binding, and checks the withdrawing guardian and live membership in the learner's tenant before mutation. The service accepts an actor and consent ID. It derives the intent's scope and generation from persisted records. [Canonical withdrawal](../../backend/modules/identity/consent.py)
- Access and withdrawal share the live consent predicate. It matches the learner, purpose, grant and expiry bounds, withdrawal, active guardian, guardian membership in the learner's tenant, verified and unrevoked relationship, and nonempty verification, notice and evidence references. The role-specific access path still narrows guardians to their own grants. Withdrawal captures one time under the learner lock and evaluates the predicate before and after saving when the first withdrawal removes a qualifying tutoring grant. [Shared predicate](../../backend/modules/identity/consent_policy.py) · [Access](../../backend/modules/access/service.py)
- A surviving qualifying consent for the withdrawing guardian suppresses the intent. If only another guardian's qualifying consent survives, the saved uploader is the withdrawing guardian. If no qualifying tutoring consent survives, the scope is learner-wide for tutoring across courses and editions. Ineffective and reporting-only first withdrawals retain the existing generation fence without a tutoring cleanup intent. A replay exits without changing the generation or creating an intent, including for legacy rows with no intent. [Canonical withdrawal](../../backend/modules/identity/consent.py) · [Founder decision](../../sources/consent-cleanup-policy-instruction.md)
- The intent pins the consent, learner, tenant, tutoring purpose, optional uploader, inclusive pre-bump generation cutoff, `effective-loss-v1` and `full_purge`. The one-to-one consent binding and policy constraint are reflected in the additive migration. The atomic boundary contains withdrawal, the generation fence and the intent insert. Allocation already takes the same learner lock and refreshes authority before creating the upload, so a later post-regrant creation falls above the recorded cutoff. Any future consent or adjacent eligibility mutation that affects selection must participate in the same serialization discipline. [Intent model](../../backend/modules/operations/models.py) · [Migration declaration](../../backend/modules/operations/migrations/0005_consent_cleanup_intent.py) · [Allocation](../../backend/modules/ingestion/service.py)

## Remaining evidence

The intent records prospective authority to remove the source and manifest for eligible uploads. A future consumer must verify withdrawn consent and reconcile all tenant, learner, purpose, uploader and cutoff bindings before acting. It must prove physical removal, writer closure, retry durability and once-only accounting. A model's noneditable fields and foreign keys alone do not prove the cross-record bindings. Qualified PostgreSQL must still test transaction rollback and lock ordering against the final implementation. Minimum retained intent, upload and consent/audit fields, their retention and access, backup policy and real-data clearance remain with G3/privacy. R046, RISK-F3-005 and F3 remain open.
