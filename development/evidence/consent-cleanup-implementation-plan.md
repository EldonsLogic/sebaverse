# DEV-010 consent withdrawal cleanup plan

21 November 2026 · R046 · RISK-F3-005 · Proposed for founder decision and independent design review. Base `ed4d8404d627ff100cac31bff7311c4e044f49fc`. The risk is that private material and its quota charge survive canonical consent withdrawal. Current exposure is limited to authored fixtures. This planning slice is limited to one document covering scope, persistence, purge and accounting. ## Decisions before implementation

**Originals and derivatives.** Choose whether eligible withdrawal should purge both the accepted private original (`blob`) and its derived `manifest`, or purge the manifest while retaining an accepted original in inaccessible storage for an approved purpose. Recommend both for this private-upload path. It removes stranded source content and matches the existing explicit uploader deletion behavior. R046 expressly requires derived purge. The architecture describes whole deletion, and the privacy spec leaves the retention schedule to G3. Neither resolves what canonical purpose withdrawal authorizes for originals. The derived-only option needs a source retention purpose, charge/visibility rules and a separately authorized route to eventual deletion. Under either option, rejected or abandoned bytes that never became an accepted source follow failed-write cleanup. [R046](../../registers/requirements.json) · [Curriculum and RAG](../../docs/04-curriculum-and-rag.md) · [API deletion contract](../../architecture/api-and-event-contracts.md) · [Retention and deletion](../../docs/06-safety-privacy-security.md#retention-and-deletion)

**Overlapping grants and ineffective records.** Consent is scoped to learner and purpose, and there is no consent-record binding on an upload. A learner's access can rely on any qualifying guardian grant, while a guardian's access requires a qualifying grant of their own. Canonical withdrawal can also transition a record that no longer qualifies for live access. Recommend scheduling learner-wide cleanup across tutoring courses/editions when the withdrawn record was qualifying and no qualifying tutoring grant remains. If another guardian’s tutoring grant remains, recommend scoping cleanup to uploads by the withdrawing guardian only when that guardian has no other qualifying grant. A redundant same-guardian grant, expired or unverified record, or unrelated purpose would schedule no cleanup. The broader alternative is learner-wide cleanup on every first withdrawal of a qualifying tutoring consent, even when another guardian still authorizes tutoring and owns material. Ineffective, duplicate and unrelated-purpose records remain excluded under both presented options. That alternative requires explicit approval for deleting uploads that may have relied on a different record. Existing learner-wide generation fencing still denies old uploads after any first canonical withdrawal, even if the chosen cleanup rule preserves some files. A new grant does not restore those old objects or imply their removal. [Canonical withdrawal](../../backend/modules/identity/consent.py#L9) · [Consent predicate](../../backend/modules/access/service.py#L26)

**Minimal receipt and retained fields.** Confirm permissible receipt fields, access and retention under the existing G3 review. Use only the scope link, pre-bump cutoff, coarse outcome and operational scheduling state necessary for replay and review. Specify what remains on the upload and consent records after successful purge and how operational history expires. The current terminal upload row still carries a free-text rights claim, hashes, command/idempotency data, scoped IDs and review provenance. Its status alone does not establish minimization. Review clearing content-bearing fields and limiting tombstone fields while preserving terminal idempotency, scope and legitimate audit needs. Record any allowed exception explicitly. No audit period, retention timetable or backup deletion deadline is proposed here. ## Authority and persistence

1. Preserve the internal `identity.consent.withdraw_consent(actor, consent_id)` entry point and its actor authorization. Compute the approved cleanup selector from persisted verified consent facts while holding the learner lock. For an effective-loss policy, evaluate the same qualifying consent predicate as access and use one timestamp to compare grants before and after withdrawal. The transition must share the learner lock with any future canonical grant or other lifecycle changes that affect this decision. Direct test-record writes are fixtures. Capture the generation `g` before increment, and commit consent withdrawal, generation fencing and any cleanup intent together. A failed commit leaves all three unchanged. Identical withdrawal replay neither increments generation nor extends scope to new uploads. Reporting consent continues existing access fencing and schedules no tutoring purge.
2. Add a narrow internal consent cleanup intent, unique by canonical consent record, with fixed tenant/learner, purpose, optional uploader filter for the overlap policy, and inclusive upper bound `upload.authorization_generation <= g`. Bind the approved purge choice to the intent so a configuration change cannot widen a queued deletion. Only the canonical transition writes a new intent. A worker resumes committed intents independently of the initiating guardian's account or relationship status and absent live tutoring consent. It verifies the intent's withdrawn-consent binding and fails closed on inconsistent state. An uploader’s manual withdrawal still requires uploader authority. No public deletion endpoint, arbitrary scope input or worker authority to read content is needed.
3. Use a dedicated persistence record with the existing database-backed queue pattern. The current `operations.Job` requires a live authorized actor/resource, mock provider request and usage reservation, and cancels on revoked scope. Reusing it would couple cleanup to the authority it must act after. Put the new intent and scheduling service in operations; keep target enumeration, file I/O and accounting in ingestion. A bounded internal entry point such as `ingestion.cleanup.run_batch(store, limit=…)` discovers committed intents after restart. `on_commit` may wake a worker as an optimization, with the persisted intent as the source of pending work. [Operations model](../../backend/modules/operations/models.py#L7) · [Operations enqueue/claim](../../backend/modules/operations/service.py#L40)
4. Revalidate each target inside its existing lifecycle locks. Match the upload's saved learner, course and purpose binding with its quota/resource and the intent’s tenant and learner. Allocation already proves the upload began as a file. Apply the exact optional actor filter to its original saved scope; do not require the mutable resource kind, purpose or edition still to authorize processing. Do not narrow by the currently active course/edition, enrollment, entitlement or new consent. They may have changed since the upload. If persisted bindings disagree, no storage deletion or refund occurs for that row, and the scope stays unresolved with a redacted integrity outcome. Preserve uploads above the cutoff, other learners and tenants, and unrelated purposes. Use only immutable server object IDs as storage keys, independent of filenames and content hashes. A post-regrant allocation with a fresh command key has a fresh object ID and a newer generation. A replay of an old command key cannot revive the prior upload. Prior withdrawn consent rows have no intent/cutoff and get no automatic retrospective sweep. [Allocation and generation](../../backend/modules/ingestion/service.py#L43) · [Private object keys](../../backend/modules/ingestion/storage.py#L17)

## Purge, writer and retry sequence

1. Claim a due intent in a short transaction with an expiring lease, fence and persistent bounded-sweep progress. Commit before taking ingestion locks. Require the worker entry point to run outside an enclosing transaction. Use deterministic keyset batches so an active writer or failed object cannot block later targets. At the end of a sweep, rescan unresolved targets on the next scheduled pass. Scope processing remains pending until every target has verifiably finished. An expired worker lease permits another worker to resume; it does not prove a file writer has closed. Keep bounded batches and bounded automatic retry/backoff per policy. Persist a redacted blocked outcome at exhaustion and support restricted operator replay of the same intent after reconciliation. A cursor advance, lease expiry or attempt cap cannot count as a purge.
2. Under the existing quota → upload → learner lock order, recheck the binding, then set `withdrawn` and increment upload generation once. Skip terminal targets. Commit before storage I/O. Withdrawal denies current authority immediately through the learner fence and prohibits future writes before purge. An active source writer retains its reservation; it must close, acknowledge closure under the lifecycle locks and never promote bytes after the fence. A crash with unknown writer ownership stays blocked and charged until controlled recovery can prove the writer cannot still create or modify the object. Do not clear `writer_active` or release quota solely by age.
3. Preserve the current manifest-writer serialization for the initial bounded cleanup. `process` creates the manifest under quota/upload/learner lifecycle locks. The cleanup consumer takes those same locks only after claiming and committing its intent lease, waits for a current manifest operation to finish, marks the target withdrawn, and commits before its own file deletion. The existing publication fence prevents a later parser from starting a manifest write for that withdrawn dispatch. Source writers still run outside locks and need the explicit closure acknowledgement above. Do not claim that a persisted writer bit proves a crashed process has closed. Moving manifest I/O outside locks would require a separate durable writer/reservation design and is not part of this first cleanup implementation. Cleanup's own filesystem operations must stay outside transactions.
4. Once no writer remains, run idempotent object deletion outside all transactions. For full purge, remove both suffixes, including incomplete objects, and require successful absence confirmation before settlement. The local adapter tolerates already missing objects. If an unlink partially succeeds and then fails, keep all relevant holds until a retry proves the approved set is absent. Reacquire the lifecycle locks, verify the same terminal transition/binding, subtract the row’s remaining used bytes (`stored_bytes + manifest_bytes`) and reservations, zero those counters and mark `deleted` atomically. A stale execution cannot refund twice. After a crash between unlink and settlement, replay confirms absence and performs the one remaining refund. Validate that the approved adapter’s completion guarantee supports any claimed crash durability. [Local purge](../../backend/modules/ingestion/storage.py#L60)
5. For derived-only purge, use a fixed manifest-only storage operation and a distinct terminal outcome such as `withdrawn_derived_purged`. Release only confirmed derived charges and holds for absent abandoned objects. Keep any accepted original inaccessible and charged under the approved policy. Preserve eligibility for later explicit uploader removal, and ensure an overlapping full purge wins without a second refund. Never represent this outcome as whole-object deletion.

## Independent review follow-ups

The [review](consent-cleanup-plan-review.md) accepts this as a decision proposal, not implementation acceptance. Root selected preservation of existing manifest serialization for the bounded first implementation. Persistent tracking must retain blocked early targets across keyset page boundaries and repeated sweeps; a cursor cannot imply completion. Local unlink success or one absence check is not proof against host/power-loss resurrection. Before terminal deletion and final credit can claim such durability, qualify directory synchronization or an equivalent approved storage guarantee and reconcile interrupted deletion. The required runtime evidence remains pending. No native gate, policy or retained-record timetable was changed.

## 22 November implementation clarification

AUTH-015 targets old private uploads across versions. The consumer therefore validates original saved learner/course/purpose, quota and resource learner/course identities against the intent; current resource kind, purpose, edition and current entitlements may deny reads but do not change which old file is being purged. Identity disagreements block deletion/credit. Terminal local confirmation requests directory synchronization and checks both names absent; it is not certification of hardware power-loss, backups or qualified remote-store durability. Unknown abandoned writers remain pending/charged without a proof-bearing reset. No real database or production runtime was used.
