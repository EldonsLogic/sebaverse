# Independent local writer recovery design review

- **Close the gap before file creation.** The receiver acquires the shared guard before committing `receiving` and its exact active attempt. It rechecks the live row and scope while holding the guard. A receiver that resumes after the fence fails the eligibility check before opening a file. A receiver that already committed its attempt prevents exclusive acquisition until it can no longer open or write the source. Keep every protected source descriptor inside the guard lifetime, including failures during descriptor wrapping. Use the pinned directory for the source open, exceptional unlink and recovery purge. Clear an active binding on normal completion or fenced acknowledgment only for the exact attempt that owned the guard. Neither elapsed time, a PID nor absence of a file establishes closure.
- **Verify the actual inode and local protocol.** Binding the root and stable stripe to the persisted attempt excludes a missing, newly created or substituted lock from authorizing recovery. Confirm ownership, type and link count under the trusted private root. Keep stripe files through cleanup and retries. The claim relies on a stable same-host namespace and coherent advisory locks, with every protected receiver using this protocol and never transferring source descriptors outside its guard. Legacy active rows and unsupported, mismatched or malformed bindings stay charged. Stripes carry no per-upload metadata. Same-stripe contention can conservatively defer a row and requires bounded revisits.
- **Keep deletion and quota fencing intact.** An already authorized withdrawal or consent intent commits the scope-specific fence first. Release lifecycle locks before opening or acquiring the recovery guard. Verify the exact attempt, withdrawn generation and applicable authority again before purge and settlement. Preserve quota until strict original-and-manifest confirmation and a matching settlement transaction. A stale lease, a legitimate concurrent owner acknowledgment or an identity mismatch invalidates the captured attempt. Retry legitimate state changes under fresh authority. Keep the existing manifest transaction fence and post-regrant isolation.

Require deterministic tests for the delayed pre-commit receiver and the marked receiver paused before `open`, live contention and abrupt exit before or during source write, wrong root or replaced inode, legacy active rows, lease takeover and partial purge replay. Include an independently opened shared holder and exclusive contender. Exercise the stable lock when a contender has opened the old inode before attempting acquisition, and ensure replacement never becomes proof. Verify eventual exact-once credit after a legitimate owner acknowledgment or a process crash. A late manifest write must also fail the existing withdrawal fence.

**Observed evidence.** On Darwin with Python 3.9.6, a standard-library probe acquired two independent shared locks and confirmed that the live child holder blocked an exclusive attempt. Unlinking and recreating the pathname let the probe acquire a false exclusive lock on the new inode while the old inode remained blocked. The old inode accepted the exclusive lock after termination and reap of the authored child. The probe used a temporary directory and exited 0. `make foundation-check` exited 0 with artifact and foundation validation, economics recalculation and 179 fixture tests. The application recovery implementation, PostgreSQL, worker runtime composition and Linux/storage qualification were pending at this review. The probe establishes local lock behavior. F3 and RISK-F3-005 remain open.
