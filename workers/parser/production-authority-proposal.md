# DEV-010 production parser authority proposal

23 November 2026 · R039/R040/R041/R046 · RISK-F3-004 · source baseline `d8a1509`. This design revision responds to the [21 November independent review](production-authority-review.md). A weak authority boundary could expose another learner’s private file, admit a forged scan verdict or publish after withdrawal. Current evidence covers authored fixtures. The budget is one design pass and two review corrections. Revised design review, ADR disposition and runtime qualification remain pending. ## Current boundary

The broker checks a server-owned authored-fixture asset registry, a three-field scanner reply and the active dispatch. Its parse recheck runs under quota → upload → learner row locks. Those locks are released before calling the adapter. Live reads cover actor and resource scope, but those reads do not serialize every change to the underlying authority. Canonical consent withdrawal bumps learner authorization generation, and upload withdrawal and the cleanup consumer fence upload generation. Neither those mechanisms nor same-host recovery of a source writer provides a worker capability gate. The PDF core verifies HMAC job and scan envelopes with separate fixture keys. A shared HMAC key lets every holder sign. ## Trusted roles and evidence

| Role | Proposed authority |
| --- | --- |
| Ingestion broker | Derives authority from committed server state. Issues the signed job, verifies the independent scan attestation and validates the result. The broker is trusted to honor the independent verdict. |
| Release coordinator and launcher | A single trusted, same-host authority owner serializes release and invalidation. The launcher authenticates the broker request and verifies the job against the coordinator’s immutable active record. It provisions isolated invocations, holds source descriptors and performs release. It requests scanner work on the verified dispatch and consults the coordinator’s verified clean-scan record for parser release. A valid broker signature alone establishes neither current scope nor scan clearance. |
| Trusted scanner controller | Accepts only an authenticated launcher grant for one scanner stage of one dispatch. It selects the approved scanner binding and attests the independently measured input and successful complete verdict of that exact invocation. It runs outside the native scanner process. |
| Trusted supervisor and provisioner | Authenticates pinned broker, launcher and scanner-controller peers through qualified local channels, provisions per-dispatch credentials, enforces limits and owns process-tree teardown even if the broker dies. The roles may share a trusted service boundary. Their channels and privileges still require qualification. |
| Native scanner and parser | Each reads only its one released source handle. Neither receives signing or verification keys, signature envelopes, launcher authority, learner database access, storage lookup, private mounts or provider access. Scanner/parse output is bounded and untrusted. |

The broker captures exactly eight job fields under the dispatch fence: object ID, source SHA-256, upload generation, dispatch ID, parser identity, media type, byte count and scope digest. The digest is a version-tagged hash of canonical broker-derived tenant, actor, learner, resource, purpose, course, curriculum edition and version, and learner authorization generation. Retain the authoritative fields inside the broker. The digest supplies an exact scope binding and confers no permission. Pin the reviewed runtime registry identity with the dispatch. The immutable scanner identity resolves to the approved executable and runtime closure, rules/signature data and scan policy revisions. Unknown, disabled or changed bindings block use of the old dispatch.

For the proposed HMAC continuation, the provisioner independently generates two distinct dispatch-scoped keys of at least 32 bytes with a reviewed OS randomness source. Deliver them only over authenticated, confidential, supervisor-controlled local channels scoped to the exact dispatch and trusted endpoint identity. The job key goes to the broker and launcher. The scan key goes to the scanner controller and broker. The provisioner and either holder can mint the corresponding HMAC, including the broker's ability to forge a scan with its verification key. This design depends on trusted broker behavior and does not supply third-party proof against a dishonest broker. Bind provisioning to the immutable endpoint identities, dispatch, scope, registry and deadline. Reject replaced peers or credentials, revoked registry bindings, expired keys and notes from previous supervisor instances. Fence affected dispatches on revocation and remove key copies after teardown. Provisioning, peer authentication, key lifecycle and mandatory revocation routing are unqualified. If they are unavailable, release stays disabled.

The launcher sends the scanner controller an authenticated, one-dispatch scan grant derived from the verified job, including the eight expected fields, exact approved scanner binding and deadline. The controller independently measures hash and count from the same sealed source and confirms that the designated scan consumed the complete input required by policy. It accepts a clean verdict only from the dedicated channel for that supervised executable and invocation. Partial, timed-out, skipped, unsupported, dirty or indeterminate scans, native failure or unpinned rule drift produce no clean attestation. The controller signs only the exact eight-field job binding plus approved scanner ID, clean status and expiry, using the existing scan envelope shape. The broker validates against its own snapshot and verifies the scanner and deadline before recording clearance. Decode both envelopes and control messages with bounded exact field/type/value checks before canonicalization. The attestation establishes provenance of the measured invocation and verdict. It depends on the qualified scanner for detection and cannot establish that a compromised scanner told the truth. The existing unsigned adapter result and fixture keys do not demonstrate this authority.

## Once-only release and invalidation

The proposed first composition uses **one trusted authority gate for all invalidating commits and actual capability release on the same host**. Take this gate before any lifecycle row locks, including the intent lock after quota → upload → learner where needed. Hold it across each committed invalidating transaction and each short release decision through the actual capability transfer. The endpoint that makes the descriptor readable must itself own the gate until transfer completes. A separate launcher can never rely on an earlier message that a coordinator held the gate. Stage, scan, parse, drain result pipes and tear down outside the gate and row transactions. This gate is a new required design change. Row-lock checks alone leave a gap after commit and cannot fence a local send if a database session drops. The local gate must continue excluding every invalidator across the verified database read and local transfer. A lost or ambiguous database decision blocks release, and an unresolved invalidating commit blocks further releases until its outcome is reconciled.

Route every scope-invalidating writer through the gate and canonical transactions. Each committed invalidation must irreversibly stale all affected open dispatches, or bump a checked generation that has the same effect. A later regrant cannot revive them. The minimum coverage includes:

- Cancel, upload withdrawal/deletion, cleanup fences, retries, dispatch replacement, lease or owner loss, and source, generation or resource-binding changes.
- Actor or learner disablement, tenant activation changes, membership and role changes, and relationship verification changes or revocation. Include supporting guardians whose active status, membership or relationship makes their consent qualify, and assignments wherever a teacher is an authorized actor.
- Consent grant changes, replacements and withdrawal. Enrollment, entitlement purpose or window changes, revocation, quota exhaustion, course activation or tenant change, resource purpose/kind/visibility, curriculum edition withdrawal or version drift, and any applicable source-rights or scanner/parser approval change.
- Timed expiry checked independently at the release and acceptance edge. Use the original selected qualifying consent and entitlement witnesses. Their expiry or another required time cutoff cannot be extended by a replacement witness within the same dispatch.

The current learner generation handles the canonical consent fence only. Do not assume it covers the other writers. Before enabling a target, enumerate and restrict all mutation routes, including Django admin, maintenance jobs, service accounts, registry reloads, alternate application processes and direct SQL. If an applicable write path can bypass the gate, shared state is updated from another host, or an uncertain mutation cannot be reconciled, that runtime remains closed. Gate scope is intentionally coarse for this candidate. It does not establish a production multi-host design.

1. Create a fresh dispatch and fixed deadline under the gate and lifecycle locks. Reserve a bounded staging/cleanup obligation. Read only the source pinned by that dispatch, with the stored size, PDF signature/type and SHA-256 checked against the existing 8 MiB ingress ceiling. Seal it outside the gate. Prepare one source-free isolated receiver per stage. The supervisor must be able to destroy a receiver with a queued descriptor.
2. Commit one durable, owner-bound claim per dispatch and engine stage before attempting to transfer anything. Use a unique stage record and forbid its reissue. A claim is spent on owner loss or an ambiguous send. It grants no custody by itself. For both scanner and parser, reacquire the gate for release, reread current scope, dispatch, owner, stage, registry and deadline under the lifecycle fence, and revalidate the exact input handle. Parser release also rechecks the verified scan bound to this dispatch and the same sealed object. Keep the gate through the actual transfer.
3. The authorized launcher consumes its one local send opportunity immediately before a single bounded, nonblocking transfer to the dedicated receiver. The point of release is the first moment the receiver can obtain the readable descriptor. For a direct Unix `SCM_RIGHTS` handoff this is successful enqueue to a channel readable by that receiver, including a queued descriptor awaiting its first read. A receiver acknowledgment is evidence of delivery only. Anything that queues the descriptor before the fence, or checks authority only when issuing a ticket, fails the design. With an intermediary, apply the gate at the final step that makes the descriptor readable to the isolated receiver. On timeout, blocked send, owner crash, partial or uncertain outcome, abandon the dispatch, fence it and close the channel. The launcher never retries the same claim.
4. Committed withdrawal before the actual transfer denies it. Withdrawal ordered after release first marks the dispatch stale and records a stop obligation, then the supervisor stops the engine and closes queued and open handles. Bytes already read remain exposed to the invocation until teardown. Verify termination and handle closure before allowing retry. If the gate owner fails, the supervisor fences the owner, drains its channels and reaps its engines before enabling a replacement. Recovery abandons ambiguous stage records and starts a new dispatch with a fresh source handle, credentials and scan. A prior authentic scan or result never creates a second launch or commit. [Unix descriptor transfer semantics](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man7/unix.7.html)

## Fixed expiry and final acceptance

Issue and persist an absolute dispatch deadline at creation, bounded by the trusted issuance time plus at most 120 seconds and the earliest applicable authority cutoff. Both signed envelopes must expire at or before that deadline. Never recompute the outer window from verification time. Before scanner release use the earlier of job and dispatch expiry. Before parser release and final acceptance include the scan expiry. Compare the current trusted clock at the actual release edge and within the final acceptance fence, using strict before-expiry comparisons. Also arm a monotonic elapsed budget at creation for staging, scanning, launch and bounded pipes, with each stage clamped to the remaining dispatch budget. Wall-clock rollback cannot extend it. Separate bounded teardown continues after the cutoff. Unknown clock state, budget or supervisor epoch denies release. Qualification must demonstrate the real deadline at the point of readability, including suspend or delay between a clock read and descriptor enqueue. A pre-check followed by an unbounded handoff cannot establish this guarantee. Keep release disabled if the chosen mechanism cannot bound or enforce that edge.

The parser verifies source size, type and hash before invoking the engine and emits only the current bounded result schema. The broker retains the eight-field binding and independently checks the six-field output provenance against the result from that invocation. Under the same authority gate and lifecycle fence, recheck exact dispatch, freshness, scan, live permissions, accepted scope and available quota. Stage the validated result outside the gate in dispatch-private storage before the final transaction. A short final conditional transition under the fence publishes the reference, charges quota and sets `needs_review` together. Only that committed state makes the manifest reachable through the broker. Final acceptance must qualify the deadline at visible commit, including delays after the last in-transaction clock check. A time check that can be followed by an arbitrarily delayed publish is insufficient. Keep publication disabled if the chosen commit boundary cannot enforce the cutoff. This requires future staging/reference/accounting changes to the existing file write under row locks. A failure or cancellation cleans the staged object or leaves a charged, discoverable cleanup obligation. No duplicate or stale attempt publishes. Existing scoped rights/extraction review remains necessary for private readiness. ## Two-engine input and cleanup qualification

The Linux candidate is one anonymous sealed file per dispatch. Establish and verify full write, grow and shrink seals with the seal set fixed. A future-write seal alone can leave an existing writable shared mapping. Close writable descriptors and mappings before handoff. Open separate read-only file descriptions for scanner and parser, each at offset zero, and verify the same device/inode, size, type, access mode and seals for each. Descriptor duplication can share seek position, so independent opening or a proven exclusively positional-read convention is required. Check handle identity again at each release. The scanner controller independently measures the same sealed bytes without advancing either engine's position. Any trusted descriptor-opening mechanism must stay outside both engine isolation boundaries. The parser receives only the one source descriptor and required bounded metadata. [Linux anonymous files](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man2/memfd_create.2.html) · [Linux file seals](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man2/F_ADD_SEALS.2const.html) · [Unix open-file descriptions](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man7/unix.7.html)

Both invocations need independently reviewed immutable code and import roots, restricted process identity, private mounts, no access to parent or peer processes, no extra inherited handles or shared control sockets, and enforced filesystem, network, memory, CPU, process and time limits. The supervisor must close all copies of source handles and control channels, prove both invocation trees empty and remove staged artifacts on success, failure, expiry, cancellation and controller loss. A cgroup v2 candidate needs confinement and proof of empty ownership, including concurrent descendants. The existing watchdog alone cannot establish this boundary. Review memory copies, swap, dumps, diagnostics and recovery artifacts. Keep original and derived-file deletion on the existing authorized cleanup path. Track the separate dispatch staging obligation until proven removed and retain the applicable hold while material may survive. An incomplete teardown is a blocked state, even if the main child exited. [Linux cgroup v2](https://docs.kernel.org/admin-guide/cgroup-v2.html#core-interface-files) · [Fixture process guard](process-guard-contract.md)

## Required evidence and decision

- Race every invalidator and natural expiry before actual scanner and parser readability, including after durable claim and while the broker is paused. Require zero release if the invalidator won. If release won, require bounded teardown and zero stale manifest acceptance. Exercise disconnect, uncertain commit, duplicate verdict, missing receipt acknowledgment, crash during send and replacement-owner recovery.
- Reject wrong program, rules, scan policy, peer and credentials. Reject incomplete verdict, stale/replayed dispatch, each mismatched field, and late acceptance. Inspect both processes for wrong, writable or repositioned descriptors, writable aliases, cross-job access, extra handles, surviving descendants, private logs and spill. Confirm teardown and once-only quota behavior on normal exit and every failure boundary.
- Keep `digital_pdf.run_job`, authored SDP1 framing, signed preflight, disabled default and bounded result compatibility. The proposal moves signed checks outside the native engines. Adopt that change only through a reviewed ADR disposition before composition. Current scanner and launcher qualification, PostgreSQL and runtime validation, actual R039 fidelity and F3 exits still require separate evidence. AUTH-014 supplier wait remains in force.
